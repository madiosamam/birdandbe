2. How bad output got caught on this run

  • Template bleed. I wrote “2-minute quiz” in 7 places out of habit from the Golden Child set. Bird&Be says “five minutes”. Caught when the dossier came in. Rule from this: build only from the brand’s dossier, never from the previous set, and run a claim check that flags any number not found in the dossier.
  • Ingredient error. I put NAC in both packs; the facts panel lists it only in hers. Same check.
  • The brand’s own inconsistencies were caught by the research step: “52.5%” on product pages vs “52.2%” in Bird&Be’s study release; NAC in the male bullets but not in the facts panel. These went on the claims sheet as “hold until fixed”. This is the kind of thing a client notices first.
  • Visual QA (screenshots, not eyeballing): cropped product shots, chips stretching full width, two-column sections not stacking on mobile, an ad whose product photo didn’t work split in half.
  • Compliance: testimonials that describe getting pregnant were deliberately excluded (fertility is a sensitive, Meta-restricted category).
  • A second, independent fact-check. After the first QA, a separate agent re-read every line of the set against the dossier, the product data and the timing log. It found 6 errors the first pass missed: the creator ramp and weekly ad counts didn’t match between tabs, a monthly figure labelled as one week, a $0.01 rounding error, an accuracy claim attached to the wrong test, and step times in this write-up that didn’t match my own log. It also found about a dozen smaller issues: two reviewers labelled “Verified buyer” where the site says “Anonymous”, two reviews from a pregnancy product on a pre-conception page, and wording that went beyond what Bird&Be states about its quiz. A click-through found one navigation bug. All fixed before sending. Rule from this: the fact-check runs as its own step, by an agent that didn’t write the set.

What runs in parallel: research for sets N+1…N+4 runs while set N is being built and reviewed. With 4 in flight, 20 sets is about 4–5 hours of machine time. Human time is about 20 × 15 min = 5 hours, so the day is one operator’s full shift.

4. Where Claude’s limits hit, and the fix

  • Research is the expensive step. One dossier used 290K tokens and 110 tool calls, mostly the model reading raw HTML; the fact-check pass used another 188K. At 20 a day that’s about 5.8M tokens on research alone, and it eats the 5-hour and weekly windows first.
  • Fix 1: scripts before the model. Shopify products.json, Judge.me/Okendo review widgets, JSON-LD ratings and theme colours can be pulled by a plain script and handed to Claude as clean JSON. I’d expect this to cut research tokens by more than half (estimate; I’d measure it in week one).
  • Fix 2: a smaller model for collection and checking, the big model for writing. Sub-agents that fetch, extract and compare lines against the dossier don’t need the top model.
  • Fix 3: freeze the kit. The shell, CSS and 12 ad layouts are written once; each set only writes brand-specific content.

All three fixes stay inside Claude Code: Claude writes the collection scripts, runs them, and picks the model for each sub-agent. None of them needs a paid tool.

Honest number: on one Max account with no changes, I’d expect the usage windows to cap this at roughly 12–15 sets a day at this quality. With fixes 1–3, 20 a day is realistic on the same account, and the limit moves to human review time. I’d confirm both numbers with the usage meter on your account on day one; the per-set token figures above are from this run, the daily caps are my estimate.
