03 · The work itself

What a roster actually ships

TikTok and Instagram to start. A hook in the first two seconds, shot on a phone in a real kitchen or bathroom, nothing that looks like an ad. The format stays; the content changes by stage.

“Our morning stack, both of us”

Couple opens two weekly bags side by side. Hook: “He takes one too.” Lines from the sheet only.

“The test he did at home”

Partner unboxes the At-Home Sperm Test Kit, walks through it, no results claims. Hook: “We tested both of us, not just me.”

“What’s actually in a prenatal”

Reads the label out loud: CoQ10, choline, folate form, as stated. Hook: “I compared 3 prenatal labels.”

“Day 1 of trying, together”

Routine vlog. Shows the quiz on the phone. No timelines, no outcomes.

“Gummies I don’t have to force down”

Taste test of the new Gentle Prenatal Gummies (Sour Cherry Lime). Pregnancy-stage accounts.

“Things nobody told my husband”

The male side, in the partner’s own words, approved lines only. The category’s least-used angle.

05 · Sourcing & vetting

Where they come from, and five filters

Four sources in

1. Bird&Be’s own reviewers and subscribers: people who already wrote about their journey.
2. TTC and IVF TikTok / Instagram: screen for people already filming routines.
3. UGC marketplaces: fastest route to a month-one cohort.
4. Paid recruiting ads pointed at an application page: the only source we control the volume of.

Five filters, fewer each step

1. Portfolio: three delivered videos.
2. Claims test: one brief from the sheet, scored on staying inside it.
3. Trial month: full deliverables, real deadlines.
4. Score: reliability, craft, compliance.
5. Retainer: winners continue; the rest are cut, in writing.

Bird&Be already runs an affiliate program (10% commission, “400+ affiliates”, via Impact) and has a professionals program for clinicians. The first recruits come from there. No named creators are listed here on purpose: nobody has been checked for existing deals or exclusivity yet, and this category deserves that check before a name goes on a page.

Bird&Be · draft v0.1 for legal sign-off · 1 Oct 2026

So the claims sheet comes first, and every ad, landing page and creator brief on this site is written from it. Green lines are copied from Bird&Be’s own packaging and site; health benefit lines always carry the FDA asterisk. Nothing runs in paid until your team signs this off.

Yellow: on the site today, held until fixed or substantiated

LineIssue found
“Clinically studied to improve progressive sperm motility by 52.5% after 3 months*”The product pages say 52.5%; Bird&Be’s own study release (Sept 2024) and blog say 52.2%. Pick one, keep the footnote (“in a clinical study of males with infertility”), and have the study on file before it runs in paid.
Male Fertility Power Pack: “antioxidants like NAC”The supplement-facts panel on the same page doesn’t list NAC. Check the physical label before any NAC line is used for him.
“Over 97% accuracy” (sperm test) · “Over 99.9% accuracy” (FSH test, ovulation tests)Accuracy claims need the study on file before paid. The ovulation tests also disagree: “over 99.9%” in their own copy, “over 99%” on the TTC bundle page. Pick one.
“Our ingredients actively work to help you get pregnant.”Outcome + superiority claim on the prenatal comparison page. Not for paid.
Homepage testimonials about getting pregnantCan’t be shown as typical results in ads (FTC). The reviews used on this site were chosen without outcome language.

Red: lines that never run

Never sayWhy
“Get pregnant faster” · “boosts your chances” · “it worked for us”Outcome claim. Needs clinical substantiation; a testimonial counts as the brand saying it (FTC endorsement guides)
“Struggling to conceive?” · “Is your sperm count low?”Meta personal-attributes policy: ads can’t assert or imply the viewer’s health condition
“Treats” · “cures” · “fixes” infertility, PCOS, low motilityDisease claim: turns a supplement into an unapproved drug (FDA / Health Canada)
“Better than IVF” · “instead of seeing a doctor”Medical-advice substitution; also unsafe
Numbers from a study, unless the study is on file and the line is approvedEvery number needs a source the legal team has seen
Before/after bumps, baby countdowns to cold audiencesTone: the audience includes people going through loss and IVF

How every line gets checked

  • Briefs quote the green lines word for word. Creators get the sheet with the brief, not after.
  • Automated pass: each script and caption is compared line by line against the sheet; anything that isn’t on it is flagged, not guessed at.
  • Human pass inside 24 hours for everything flagged, and a sample of what passed.
  • Testimonials in paid only use quotes without outcome language, with “individual results vary”.
  • Disclosure (#ad / Paid partnership) checked before a creator post goes live.

Draft for Bird&Be’s legal team. Policy references: Meta Advertising Standards (personal attributes; health and wellness); FTC Guides Concerning Use of Endorsements and Testimonials; FDA structure/function claim rules for dietary supplements; Health Canada NHP claims. Not legal advice.
